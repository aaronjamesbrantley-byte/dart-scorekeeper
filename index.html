How to play

The basics of a turn

Play goes in the order players are listed. On your turn you throw 3 darts, then it passes to the next player. Click where each dart lands on the board and the score updates automatically. Use Undo to take back a misclick, or Miss / outside for a dart that hit no scoring area.

Where a dart lands decides its value:

  • Big single areas — the number's face value (e.g. the 20 wedge = 20).
  • Thin outer ring (double ring) — 2× the number (double 20 = 40).
  • Thin middle ring (triple ring) — 3× the number (triple 20 = 60, the highest single-dart score).
  • Green outer bull = 25. Red inner bull (bullseye) = 50 (this counts as a double).

X01 (301 / 501 / 701 / 1001)

Everyone starts on the same number (501 is the classic). You subtract what you score each dart, racing to land on exactly zero. First to zero wins.

Bust: if a dart would take you below zero — or leave you unable to finish under the chosen rule — that whole turn is cancelled and your score goes back to where it was when the turn started.

The board shows a suggested checkout (e.g. “Checkout: T20 D20”) when you’re low enough to finish — a shortcut for what to aim at.

The finish (out) rules — how you're allowed to reach zero

  • Double out — your last dart must be a double (the thin outer ring) or the bullseye (50, which counts as a double). This is the standard tournament rule. Because of it you can never win from a score of 1 (there's no way to finish), so being left on 1 is a bust.
  • Master out — your last dart can be a double or a triple (or the bullseye). A bit easier than double out.
  • Straight out — your last dart can be anything that brings you to exactly zero, including a plain single. The simplest rule and the friendliest for beginners.
  • Double in (optional add-on) — your scoring doesn't start until you first hit a double. Darts thrown before that count as zero. It makes the opening harder; leave it off while you're learning.

Cricket

Only 7 numbers matter: 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, and the bull. The rest of the board does nothing. Your goal is to “close” each of those numbers by hitting it 3 times, and (in most variants) to score points along the way.

Each hit counts as marks — a single = 1 mark, a double = 2, a triple = 3. Three marks closes a number. On the scoreboard: / = 1 mark, X = 2 marks, Ⓧ = closed.

Once you have closed a number, any extra hits on it score its value as points — but only while an opponent still hasn't closed it. Once everyone has closed a number, it's dead and scores nothing.

The Cricket variants

  • Standard — those extra hits add points to your own score. To win you must close all 7 numbers and have the highest score. So you can't just close fast — you also can't fall behind on points.
  • Cut-throat — the twist for 3+ players: extra points get dumped onto everyone who hasn't closed that number yet. Points are bad here. You win by closing everything with the lowest score. Great for ganging up on the leader.
  • No-score — points are ignored entirely. First player to close all 7 numbers wins. The fastest, simplest version.

Training games

Practice drills — play solo to build a skill, or add players to compete. Pick one under the Training game type.

  • Around the Clock — hit every number in order, 1 through 20 then the bull. Any part of the number counts (or switch to doubles-only for a real challenge). First to clear the board wins. The best drill for learning where everything is.
  • Count-Up — throw for the highest total over a set number of rounds. No subtraction, no finishing rules — just aim at the biggest scores (the 20 up top, or the bull). A good warm-up.
  • Shanghai — each round targets one number (round 1 = the 1, round 2 = the 2, and so on). Only that number scores. Hit its single, double and triple all in the same turn and you win instantly, whatever the score. Highest total wins otherwise.
  • Bob's 27 — doubles practice. You throw 3 darts at each double in turn (D1, D2 … D20, then the bullseye). Each hit adds the double's value; miss all three and you lose that value. Drop to zero and you're out. Doubles are how you finish an X01 game, so this pays off directly.
  • Halve It — each round has a target (a number, any double, any triple, or the bull). Score on it and it adds to your total; score nothing that round and your total is cut in half. Rewards steady rounds and punishes misses.
